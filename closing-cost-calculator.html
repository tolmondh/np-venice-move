How the official rates are calculated

  • Deed documentary stamp tax: $0.70 per $100, or portion thereof, of the consideration (purchase price). Round the price up to the nearest $100, then multiply. Minimum $0.70. Sarasota County uses the statewide rate; only Miami-Dade is different. Source: Florida Department of Revenue, Documentary Stamp Tax and section 201.02, Florida Statutes.
  • Mortgage / note documentary stamp tax: $0.35 per $100, or portion thereof, of the loan amount, rounding the loan up to the nearest $100. The Clerk collects this when the mortgage is recorded. A $2,450 cap applies to certain unrecorded promissory notes under section 201.08(1)(a), but there is no cap on a mortgage recorded in Florida under section 201.08(1)(b). This estimator assumes a recorded purchase-money mortgage, so it does not apply the cap. Source: Florida DOR, Documentary Stamp Tax and section 201.08, Florida Statutes.
  • Nonrecurring intangible tax: 2 mills, which is 0.2% of the exact loan amount secured by Florida real property (do not round the loan up). The Clerk also lists this as $0.20 per $100 of the exact obligation, with a $0.20 minimum. Source: Florida DOR, Nonrecurring Intangible Tax, section 199.133, Florida Statutes, and the Sarasota County Clerk recording-fees page.
  • Owner’s title insurance (promulgated risk premium): For an original owner’s policy, $5.75 per $1,000 on the first $100,000 of liability, plus $5.00 per $1,000 from $100,000 to $1 million, plus $2.50 per $1,000 from $1 million to $5 million, plus $2.25 per $1,000 from $5 million to $10 million, plus $2.00 per $1,000 over $10 million. Any fraction of $100 is treated as a full $100. Minimum premium is $100. The owner’s policy is issued for the full insurable value, which is typically the purchase price. Lender’s (mortgagee’s) original rates use the same schedule when a lender’s policy is issued alone. Source: Fla. Admin. Code r. 69O-186.003, official rule record at flrules.org, and the Florida DFS Title Insurance Overview.
  • Simultaneous issue: When an owner’s policy and a lender’s policy on the same land are issued at the same time, the owner’s policy uses the regular owner’s rate, and the lender’s policy is a minimum $25 for coverage not exceeding the owner’s policy amount. Any lender coverage above the owner’s amount is charged at the regular original mortgage-policy rates. This estimator uses simultaneous issue whenever there is a loan. Source: Rule 69O-186.003(5) and the DFS overview linked above.
  • Reissue rates (only if you check the box): $3.30 per $1,000 on the first $100,000, plus $3.00 per $1,000 up to $1 million, with a $100 minimum, if the transaction qualifies under the rule. Confirm with the title company. Source: Rule 69O-186.003(2).
  • Sarasota County recording fees: $10.00 for the first page of each document and $8.50 for each additional page (documents 8.5" × 14" or smaller). Indexing the first four names is included; $1.00 for each additional name (not estimated here). Source: Sarasota County Clerk of the Circuit Court and County Comptroller, Recording Fees and Taxes Required.

Who usually pays what in Sarasota County?

Florida law makes the taxes due, but it does not say whether the buyer or the seller must pay the owner’s title policy or the deed documentary stamps. The Florida Department of Revenue notes that all parties to a taxable document are liable for the tax regardless of who agrees to pay it. The Florida Department of Financial Services notes that the person paying the title-insurance premium generally gets first choice of the closing or title agent, subject to the lender’s approval.

We could not find an official DOR, Administrative Code, or Clerk rule that assigns those costs to one side. In local practice in North Port and Venice, it traditionally works like this, though it is negotiable:

  • The seller pays the documentary stamp tax on the deed.
  • The seller traditionally pays the owner’s title-insurance premium (and, as the payer, usually chooses the closing agent), but this can go either way and is often negotiated in the contract.
  • The buyer pays documentary stamps on the mortgage, the intangible tax, the lender’s title policy, and the fees to record the deed and the mortgage.

That is custom, not law. Your purchase contract can flip any of it. Use the dropdowns above to match your contract, and confirm the allocation with the closing agent.

What this estimator leaves out or leaves editable

  • Title search, examination, and closing or settlement fees (related title services). Rule 69O-186.003 says these must be charged at least at actual cost and shown separately; they are not a fixed state rate.
  • A possible title insurance assessment or policy fee, which the Office of Insurance Regulation may set and which cannot exceed $25 per policy when it applies. Enter it under “other” if it appears on your quote.
  • New-home purchase discounts, substitution-loan rates, and endorsements under Rule 69O-186.003, which depend on facts this form does not collect.
  • Lender origination charges, prepaid interest, escrow deposits, owner’s insurance, surveys, WDO inspections, HOA estoppels, municipal lien searches, prorations, and attorney fees.
  • Extra Clerk indexing charges when more than four names are indexed, and any additional recorded documents (for example, a mortgage satisfaction).

This is not a Loan Estimate or Closing Disclosure. Those official forms come from your lender or closing agent.
