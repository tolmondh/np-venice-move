Florida driver license & car registration for new North Port & Venice residents

Once you move to North Port or Venice, two clocks start: one for swapping to a Florida driver license and one for titling and registering your car here. Both are handled locally by the Sarasota County Tax Collector on behalf of the state. This page lays out the order that tends to work for newcomers and the documents to have ready.

Educational overview only, not legal advice. We don't list fees or wait times because they change. Rules come from the Florida Department of Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles (FLHSMV), and local service is through the Sarasota County Tax Collector. Confirm current requirements with both before your appointment.

When you count as a Florida resident

FLHSMV says you may be considered a Florida resident when you do any of these:

  • Start a job or work in a trade or profession in Florida.
  • Enroll your children in Florida public schools.
  • Register to vote in Florida.
  • File for a homestead tax exemption on Florida property.
  • Live in Florida for more than six consecutive months.

The two deadlines

  • Driver license: within 30 days. FLHSMV says new residents must get a valid Florida driver license within 30 days of establishing residency to keep driving on Florida roads.
  • Car title and registration: within 10 days. FLHSMV says vehicles with out-of-state registrations must be registered in Florida within 10 days of the owner starting work, enrolling children in public school, or establishing residency. You'll need Florida insurance first.

Because the car deadline is shorter, many newcomers line up Florida auto insurance and a Tax Collector appointment before (or right after) moving day. Some county offices describe the timing differently, so check the Sarasota County Tax Collector's guidance for your situation.

A practical order of steps

  1. Get a Florida auto insurance policy. FLHSMV requires insurance from an agent licensed to sell insurance in Florida before it will title and register your vehicle. Bring proof that it's a Florida policy.
  2. Book an appointment. The Tax Collector recommends appointments for all services and requires them for road or written tests and for any service at the North Port office. Venice, Mid-County, and Downtown Sarasota offices also see customers. Schedule online or call the office at 941-861-8300.
  3. Switch your driver license. Bring your out-of-state license and the identity, Social Security, and residential-address documents FLHSMV lists on its What to Bring page. A Florida license with your new address is also something the Property Appraiser asks for when you apply for homestead.
  4. Title and register the car. You can often do this at the same visit. Ask whether there's a one-time fee for a vehicle that's never been registered in Florida, and budget for it.
  5. Cancel out-of-state registration and insurance only after your Florida plate and policy are active, following your old state's process for returning or surrendering plates.

What to bring for an out-of-state car (no loan)

Based on FLHSMV's new resident page (special cases may need more paperwork):

  • Proof of identity for every owner.
  • Proof of Florida insurance.
  • The original out-of-state title.
  • An Application for Certificate of Title With/Without Registration (HSMV form 82040), signed by all owners.
  • A VIN verification. The vehicle identification number has to be physically inspected, on form 82040 or the Vehicle Identification Number and Odometer Verification form (HSMV 82042), by someone FLHSMV allows, such as a law enforcement officer, a licensed Florida dealer, or a county tax collector employee. Florida law also allows a notary public. Easiest option for most people: drive the car to your appointment.

If you still have a car loan or lease

When an out-of-state lender holds your title, FLHSMV says to contact the lienholder and ask them to transfer the title to Florida. Either way, you still have to register the car. If the lender won't transfer the title, ask them for that answer in writing on letterhead and bring the letter to your appointment so you can register the vehicle. Leased cars often need paperwork from the leasing company too, so call them early.

Common newcomer mistakes to avoid

  • Showing up without a Florida insurance policy and having to come back.
  • Forgetting the original title, or not realizing the lender has it.
  • Expecting walk-in service at the North Port office, which requires an appointment.
  • Canceling the old state's insurance before the Florida policy starts.
  • Leaving the license change until after the homestead filing deadline. See our homestead exemption guide.
