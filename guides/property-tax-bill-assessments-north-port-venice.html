Your North Port & Venice property tax bill: non-ad valorem assessments explained

Sarasota County property tax bills go out in early November, and new owners are often surprised by the second half of the bill: a list of flat charges that have nothing to do with the home’s value. In North Port those charges pay for fire rescue, roads and drainage, and trash pickup. Here’s how to read them, and why the seller’s bill isn’t a forecast of yours.

Educational overview only, not tax or legal advice. Details below come from the Sarasota County Tax Collector and the City of North Port’s Property Taxes & Assessments, Fire Rescue Fees, Road and Drainage, and Solid Waste assessment pages, checked November 13, 2026. Rates are reset every year — confirm with the city or the Tax Collector before you rely on any number here.

Two halves of one bill

The Tax Collector describes the real estate tax bill as a combined notice of two different things:

  • Ad valorem taxes — based on the property’s taxable value (assessed value minus exemptions) times the millage rates set by the county, city, school board, and other taxing bodies. One mill is $1 per $1,000 of taxable value. Your homestead exemption lowers this half.
  • Non-ad valorem assessments — charges that aren’t based on value. Each levying authority uses its own unit of measure, such as per parcel or per dwelling. The Tax Collector lists stormwater, fire and rescue, and solid waste collection as typical examples. The Tax Collector only collects them; it doesn’t set them.

Because assessments aren’t tied to value, a homestead exemption doesn’t shrink them, and two homes with very different prices can carry similar assessment totals.

North Port’s three district assessments

The City of North Port says its commission sets city millage and district assessment rates during a budget process that runs from April to September, and the rates go to the Property Appraiser and Tax Collector for the November bill. The city itself doesn’t mail tax bills. North Port assessments cover the city’s fiscal year, October 1 through September 30.

Fire Rescue District

On September 10, 2026, the City Commission adopted these fire rescue rates for fiscal year 2026–27, per the city’s Fire Rescue Fees page:

  • Tier 1, response readiness — $202.26 per parcel for improved property and for unimproved parcels under 5 acres (larger unimproved parcels are charged per 5 acres).
  • Tier 2, protection of structures — $3.27 per $5,000 of structure value, rounded down to the nearest $5,000, on improved parcels.

So the fire line on a home’s bill grows with the size and value of the building, not the land. The city also notes North Port Fire Rescue holds an ISO fire suppression rating of 1/1Y, the highest rating a department can receive, which it says helps keep fire insurance costs down. Questions about the fire rates go to Fire Rescue at 941-240-8150.

Road and Drainage District

This assessment is split into several pieces: an administrative charge per parcel, base and enhanced road services per “road ERU” (equivalent residential unit), and three drainage components per “drainage EDU.” The units a property is charged depend on the city’s methodology for that parcel, so the total varies from home to home. When we checked, the city’s Road and Drainage page still listed fiscal year 2025–26 rates, so use your actual bill or call the city for the current year.

Solid Waste District

Residential trash service in North Port is paid through the tax bill rather than a monthly bill. The city’s Solid Waste page lists the residential assessment at $332.75 for fiscal year 2025–26 and says the base service includes weekly garbage, recycling, and yard waste collection plus two bulk pickups a year of up to 5 cubic yards each. See our trash & recycling guide for pickup basics.

Venice, Wellen Park, and the rest of the county

The list of assessments depends on exactly where the parcel sits: inside the City of Venice, inside North Port, or in unincorporated Sarasota County. Some master-planned neighborhoods also sit inside a community development district or similar special district (Wellen Park, for example, is served by the West Villages Improvement District), and those district charges can appear on the tax bill too. Rather than guess, pull the property’s most recent bill from the Tax Collector’s website and read each line. Each assessment line names the levying authority you can call with questions.

Paying the bill: discounts and deadlines

The Tax Collector says bills are payable starting November 1, with discounts for paying early:

  • 4% if paid in November
  • 3% in December
  • 2% in January
  • 1% in February
  • The full amount is due by March 31, and unpaid taxes become delinquent April 1.

If your mortgage includes an escrow account, the lender requests the bill and pays it, and you receive a copy. The Tax Collector says mortgage companies must pay within the 4% discount period. If you own the home outright and haven’t received a bill by November 15, the Tax Collector asks you to contact its office or print a copy from its website. Florida law makes the owner responsible for making sure the bill is paid, so update your mailing address with the Property Appraiser when you move.

What this means if you’re buying

  • Don’t budget from the seller’s total. The ad valorem half usually resets after a sale, because the seller’s homestead exemption and Save Our Homes cap don’t transfer to you. The assessment half follows the property, so it’s a better guide to what carries over.
  • Read the prior year’s actual bill, not just the listing’s “taxes” figure, and look for district or special-district lines.
  • Vacant lot in North Port? Unimproved parcels still carry fire Tier 1 and road and drainage charges each year, even before you build.
  • At closing, current-year taxes are usually prorated between buyer and seller. Try our closing cost calculator for a rough estimate, and ask your title company for actual figures.
