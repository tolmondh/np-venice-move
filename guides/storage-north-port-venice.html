Storage units during a move to North Port or Venice

Closing dates slip, rentals start late, and snowbird schedules overlap — so a lot of newcomers need storage for a few weeks or months. Here’s how to plan it for Southwest Florida’s heat, humidity, and storm season. Prices, availability, and rules change; always confirm directly with the facility and your insurer.

Educational overview only. We do not quote storage prices, rank facilities, or promise availability. Verify current terms, access hours, and contract details with each facility before you sign.

Do you actually need storage?

  • Gap between move-out and move-in — the most common reason (closing delays, a lease that starts later, a home inspection issue).
  • Downsizing or seasonal living — keeping extra furniture while you figure out what fits a Florida home or condo.
  • Renovation or flooring work — clearing rooms before you move in.
  • If the gap is only a few days, ask your mover whether they offer storage-in-transit, or consider a portable container that stays on your driveway (check HOA or deed rules first).

Climate control in a humid climate

SWFL summers are hot and humid. Non-climate-controlled units can get very warm and damp, which can matter for wood furniture, mattresses, electronics, photos, documents, musical instruments, and fabrics. “Climate controlled” isn’t a standard term — ask each facility exactly what it means for them (temperature range, humidity control, or just air movement).

  • Ask whether the unit is interior or drive-up, and whether the building is air-conditioned or humidity-controlled.
  • Store items off the floor on pallets or shelving; leave a gap from walls for airflow.
  • Make sure furniture, appliances, and fabrics are fully dry before they go in.
  • Use breathable covers instead of sealing everything in plastic, which can trap moisture.
  • Drain and dry washers, fridges, and freezers; prop doors slightly open if the facility allows.

Hurricane season and flood risk

Atlantic hurricane season runs June 1 through November 30. If you’ll be storing during that window:

  • Ask how the facility prepares for storms, whether it has closed for past events, and how access works after a storm.
  • Look up the facility address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center and check the county evacuation zone — the same steps in our flood zones primer.
  • Keep irreplaceable items (documents, photos, heirlooms) with you rather than in storage when you can.
  • Photograph and list what you store — it helps with any insurance claim.

Insurance on stored belongings

  • Many facilities require proof of coverage for stored goods. Some offer their own protection plan; that is not always the same thing as insurance.
  • Your homeowners or renters policy may cover property kept off-site, sometimes with limits. Ask your agent what applies and whether flood or wind damage is excluded.
  • Get the answer in writing, and note the facility address on your policy if required.

Questions to ask before you sign

  • What’s the current monthly rate, and how often (and by how much) can it increase? Is the first-month promo rate temporary?
  • Are there admin fees, lock requirements, or a required protection plan?
  • What are gate and office hours? Is there 24-hour access?
  • How much notice is needed to move out, and is billing prorated?
  • Can a moving truck reach the unit easily? Are there elevators or carts for upper floors?
  • What security is in place (gate codes, cameras, on-site staff)?
  • What happens to access before and after a named storm?

Sizing without guessing

Facilities usually publish size guides, but those vary. Make a quick inventory of big items (beds, sofas, dressers, appliances), measure the largest pieces, and ask the facility to recommend a size. First-month checklist

  • Confirm the move-in date, access hours, and gate code before moving day.
  • Bring the lock type the facility requires.
  • Photograph the empty unit and your stored items.
  • Set a reminder to review the bill each month and to give move-out notice on time.
  • Once you’re settled, move irreplaceable items out first.
