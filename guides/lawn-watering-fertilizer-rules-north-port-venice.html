Lawn watering days & fertilizer rules in North Port & Venice

One of the first surprises for new Southwest Florida homeowners: you can’t just run the sprinklers whenever the grass looks dry. Watering days are assigned by address, hours are limited, and both the city and county restrict fertilizer for part of the year. Here’s how to figure out which rules apply to your new home — and set your irrigation timer correctly in week one.

Educational overview only, not legal or landscaping advice. Details below come from the Southwest Florida Water Management District (SWFWMD), the City of North Port’s Water Restrictions and Conservation page, Sarasota County’s Water Restrictions and Fertilizer Management pages, the City of Venice’s water restrictions handout, and UF/IFAS Extension Sarasota County. Water shortage orders change with rainfall — always confirm the current rules with your city or county before you program a timer.

Why the rules are tight right now

North Port, Venice, and all of Sarasota County sit inside SWFWMD. After below-average rain in the 2025 and 2026 rainy seasons, the district declared a Modified Phase III “Extreme” Water Shortage, and in 2026 it extended that order through March 31, 2027. Under it, lawn watering is limited to once per week with reduced hours. SWFWMD says the restrictions apply whether your water comes from a utility, a private well, or a pond or canal.

When the shortage order ends, year-round rules still apply (still assigned days, still limited hours) — they’re just looser. Check the links at the bottom for whatever is current when you read this.

Step one: which rulebook applies to your address?

Mailing addresses can mislead. Plenty of homes with a “Venice” or “North Port” mailing address are actually in unincorporated Sarasota County. What matters is the jurisdiction and your water provider:

  • City of North Port — follows the SWFWMD schedule, enforced by the city.
  • City of Venice (inside city limits) — the city has used its own variation of the district schedule.
  • Unincorporated Sarasota County (many areas around Venice, Englewood-side, and outlying neighborhoods) — follows the county’s schedule.
  • HOA communities — the HOA may run the irrigation for you, but it must follow the same restrictions. Under the shortage order, SWFWMD says no HOA can enforce rules that would require more water use (like replacing browned-out turf for looks).

Not sure? Your utility bill shows who provides your water, and the Sarasota County Property Appraiser’s parcel record lists the taxing jurisdiction.

Your watering day (standard SWFWMD / North Port / Sarasota County schedule)

North Port and unincorporated Sarasota County both use the standard last-digit-of-address schedule:

  • House number ends in 0 or 1 → Monday
  • Ends in 2 or 3 → Tuesday
  • Ends in 4 or 5 → Wednesday
  • Ends in 6 or 7 → Thursday
  • Ends in 8 or 9, a letter, or no clear address (e.g., subdivision common areas) → Friday

Hours under the current shortage order: midnight–4 a.m. or 8–11:59 p.m. on your day. Properties under one acre may use only one of those windows. Sarasota County says properties of one acre or more may water 8 p.m.–4 a.m.

City of Venice schedule

Inside Venice city limits, the city’s handout and its April 2026 announcement assign days by even/odd house number instead: even-numbered addresses water Tuesday, odd-numbered addresses water Thursday, once per week, with sprinkler hours of 12:01–4 a.m. or 8–11:59 p.m. under the shortage order. The city has said reclaimed-water users may water any day. Confirm with Venice Utilities, since the city adjusts its local rules when the district order changes.

What’s allowed outside your day

  • Hand watering, soaker hoses, drip / micro-irrigation of plants and shrubs (not lawns) — allowed any day, but under the shortage order only before 8 a.m. or after 6 p.m.
  • Reclaimed water — SWFWMD says exclusive reclaimed-water users are subject only to voluntary hours unless the local government or utility restricts it.
  • Washing the car at home — only on your lawn watering day, with a shutoff nozzle on the hose (Sarasota County and SWFWMD).
  • Pressure washing to prep for painting or sealing is allowed under the order.

Putting in new sod or plants?

North Port’s page describes a 60-day establishment period: days 1–30, new lawns and plants may be watered any day; days 31–60, three specified days a week (even addresses Tuesday, Thursday, Sunday; odd addresses Monday, Wednesday, Saturday). Allowed hours still apply. If you’re re-sodding after closing, keep the installation receipt — it’s your proof of the install date if a code officer asks.

Check your irrigation timer and rain sensor in week one

  • Find the controller (garage wall or side of the house) and confirm the day and start times match your address’s schedule. Previous owners and winter residents often leave old settings.
  • Sarasota County requires a working rain sensor shut-off on automatic systems (Ordinance 2006-053). Make sure it’s connected and not bypassed.
  • Re-check the clock after daylight saving changes, power outages, and storms.
  • Sarasota County says it is issuing citations without a warning during the shortage, so this matters.

Fertilizer: the summer ban

Summer rain washes fertilizer into canals and bays, so local codes restrict nitrogen and phosphorus:

  • Sarasota County and the City of Venice: no fertilizer containing nitrogen or phosphorus on lawns or landscape plants from June 1 through September 30.
  • City of North Port: UF/IFAS Extension lists North Port’s restricted period as April 1 through September 30 for turf.
  • Rest of the year: lawn fertilizer must contain at least 50% slow-release nitrogen, with no more than 4 lbs of nitrogen per 1,000 sq ft per year (county code).
  • Keep fertilizer at least 10 feet from ponds, canals, and seawalls, and sweep any spills off driveways and sidewalks. (North Port’s and Venice’s exact setback rules differ slightly — see the UF/IFAS comparison.)
  • Sarasota County suggests iron and micronutrient products to keep lawns green in summer, plus mowing high and leaving clippings.

If you hire a lawn service, ask whether its technicians hold the state’s required fertilizer certification and follow your city’s restricted season.
