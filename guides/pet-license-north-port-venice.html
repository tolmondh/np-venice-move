Pet licenses, rabies tags & animal services for new North Port & Venice residents

If your dog or cat is moving with you, there's one county to-do that's easy to miss: the Sarasota County pet license tag. North Port and Venice are both in Sarasota County, and the county's animal ordinance applies in both cities, so you get one county tag no matter which side of town you land in. Here's how it works and where to check.

Educational overview only. Details below come from the Sarasota County Sheriff's Office Animal Services pages and the Sarasota County animal ordinance (Chapter 14). Fees and procedures can change, so confirm with Animal Services before you rely on them.

Who runs animal services here

The Sarasota County Sheriff's Office runs Animal Services for the whole county, including licensing, the shelter, and lost and found. The City of North Port adopted the county's animal control ordinance into its own code, and the city's Animal Services page points loose or wild animal concerns to the Sheriff's Animal Services. In Venice and the rest of the county, it's the same county ordinance and the same Sheriff's office.

What the county requires

  • Rabies vaccination: dogs, cats, and ferrets four months or older must be vaccinated for rabies by a licensed veterinarian.
  • A license tag every year: owners of dogs and cats four months or older must get a Sarasota County license certificate and tag each year. It's valid for one year from the date it's issued.
  • Three-year rabies shots still need a yearly tag: a USDA-approved three-year vaccine keeps your pet current on rabies, but the county still requires the license tag to be bought every year.
  • Wear the tag: the ordinance treats a missing tag on the collar or harness as evidence the animal isn't licensed or vaccinated. The tag color and shape change each fiscal year, and a tag can't be moved to a different pet.

Current county license fees

The Sheriff's County Ordinance Fees page lists these licensing fees:

  • Unaltered dog or cat (not spayed or neutered): $25
  • Altered dog or cat (spayed or neutered), or under one year old: $10
  • Replacement tag for a lost or damaged current-year tag: $5

The Sheriff's office says registration fees are nonrefundable and can't be transferred. Check the fee page before you pay in case amounts change.

How to get a tag as a new resident

  1. Find your rabies certificate. You need a current certificate signed by a licensed veterinarian. If your vet was out of state, the ordinance allows other verifiable proof of current vaccination that Animal Services accepts, so bring the paperwork from your old vet.
  2. Send the certificate to Animal Services. Per the Sheriff's online payments page, you can email a scan to petlicenses@sarasotasheriff.org, fax it to 941-316-1086 (attention: Pet Licenses), or mail it to Sarasota County Sheriff's Office Animal Services, Attention: Pet Licenses, 8451 Bee Ridge Rd., Sarasota, FL 34241.
  3. Pay online. The same page takes Visa, MasterCard, AMEX, or Discover. It says to enter your phone number as the account number, and if you just moved to Sarasota County or got a new pet and don't have an activity number or last year's tag number, to type "NEW" in that field.
  4. Update your address if needed. If your new address isn't on the rabies certificate, the Sheriff's office asks you to email your new address to PetLicenses@sarasotasheriff.org.
  5. Watch the mail. The Sheriff's office says the tag is mailed within 14 days of receiving your rabies certificate.

Another option: the ordinance requires participating local vets to tell you about the county license when they give a rabies shot and to offer to sell you the tag, at no more than the county fee. If you're setting up with a new local vet anyway, ask at that first visit.

If your pet gets lost

  • Report a lost animal to Animal Services at 941-861-9501. Their page says voicemail is checked regularly, including weekends and holidays, and asks for the animal's description, name, where it was last seen, and your contact details.
  • You can also email a photo and those details to lostandfound@sarasotasheriff.org.
  • To claim a pet from the shelter, the Sheriff's page says to bring a state-issued ID, driver license, or passport, plus proof of ownership (vet records, photos) and proof of current rabies vaccination and county license if you have it.
  • The Sheriff's office recommends microchipping as backup ID, and keeping current ID tags on your pet at all times. If your microchip is registered with your old address, update it with the chip company.

Sarasota County Animal Services

Animal Services is at 8451 Bee Ridge Road in Sarasota, main phone 941-861-9500. Their page lists administrative office hours of Monday through Friday, 8 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., with separate weekend hours for adoptions and lost and found. Check the Animal Services page for current hours before you drive up.

Plan for pets in hurricane season

The Sheriff's Animal Services page links to Sarasota County Emergency Services for pet disaster planning. Add your pet's rabies certificate, license number, and a recent photo to your storm kit. Our hurricane prep guide for newcomers covers the rest of the kit and evacuation basics.

Renting or in an HOA?

County rules are only part of the picture. Leases and HOA or condo documents often have their own pet rules, such as limits on number, size, or breed. Read them before you sign, and see our HOA first-month guide for what to ask.

First-month pet checklist

  1. Get copies of your pet's vet records and current rabies certificate before you move.
  2. Buy your Sarasota County license tag online or through a local vet.
  3. Put the tag on your pet's collar or harness.
  4. Update your microchip registration with your new address and phone.
  5. Save Animal Services' lost-pet number, 941-861-9501, in your phone.
  6. Add pet supplies and records to your hurricane kit.
