Well water & septic basics for North Port & Venice homebuyers

If you grew up on city water and sewer, a North Port listing that says “well and septic” can be a surprise. Many homes on North Port’s older platted lots still use a private well and an onsite septic system. That isn’t automatically a problem, but it changes what you check before you buy and what you maintain after you move in.

Educational overview only, not plumbing, engineering, or legal advice. Details below come from North Port Utilities’ Common Questions, the city’s Wastewater Long-term Expansion Plan and Mandatory Utilities Connection pages, the Florida Department of Health in Sarasota County, and the Florida Department of Health private well page. Fees, programs, and city decisions change — confirm with the utility or health department before you rely on any number here.

Step one: find out what the property actually has

Don’t assume from the neighborhood. Two houses on the same street can differ.

  • North Port addresses — North Port Utilities says buyers can use its Water & Sewer Availability Lookup (linked from the city’s Utilities page) to search by street name or parcel ID. The city says its water and sewer system maps are not public, so for connection costs call the Field Service Office at 941-240-8000.
  • Venice and unincorporated areas — The City of Venice and Sarasota County each run their own utility. Ask the seller, the listing agent, or the utility that serves the address whether the home is on central water and sewer before you write an offer.
  • Seller disclosures and utility bills — A home on city water gets a water bill. A home with no water bill is very likely on a well.

Looking up septic records

The Florida Department of Health in Sarasota County says septic permitting records from 1972 to the present have been digitized and can be searched online through its Septic Search tool, or by calling 941-861-6133. Records can show when the system was permitted and what was approved, which is useful context before an inspection.

Who handles what: since July 1, 2021, the Florida Department of Environmental Protection (DEP) is responsible for state septic rules, but the county health department office still handles septic permitting and inspections in Sarasota County.

Septic inspections when you buy

Sarasota County doesn’t require routine inspection of a standard home septic system once it’s approved, according to the Gulf Coast Community Foundation’s water quality playbook. That means no one checks it for you, so a buyer’s inspection is your chance to learn its condition. Points worth knowing:

  • The county health department says only registered septic tank contractors and state-licensed plumbers may work on septic systems in Florida.
  • A failing system needs a repair permit before repair work starts, and new or modified systems in Sarasota County must be designed by a professional engineer under county code.
  • Some homes have an aerobic treatment unit (ATU) or performance-based system instead of a conventional tank. Those need an operating permit renewed every two years and a maintenance contract, with service at least twice a year. If the listing mentions an ATU, ask the seller for the current permit and maintenance records.
  • Ask your inspector what their septic inspection does and doesn’t cover. A standard home inspection may not include it.

Testing well water

Public water systems in Florida must test routinely, but the state says private well owners are responsible for their own water. Florida doesn’t require well testing when a home is sold. The Department of Health recommends:

  • Testing for coliform bacteria and nitrate at least once a year.
  • Testing for lead about every three years for most homeowners, and including lead in a test when buying or selling.
  • Using a state-certified laboratory. DEP keeps a searchable list of certified labs.

The Florida Department of Health in Sarasota County currently lists a do-it-yourself drinking water test kit for $20, with an extra $40 for staff to collect the sample at your home in some cases. Its Venice Environmental Health office is 941-861-3310 and its Sarasota office is 941-861-6133. UF/IFAS Extension Sarasota County also notes that well owners whose wells were damaged or flooded in a hurricane may qualify for free testing supplies, and that its soil lab can test well water for things like iron, sodium, chloride, and pH (not bacteria).

North Port’s city water and sewer expansion

North Port’s Utilities Department describes a long-term plan to move platted-lot neighborhoods off septic. The first area, called Blue Ridge–Salford North, covers 1,583 properties, and the city estimated 12–15 years to finish that first neighborhood.

For homeowners who connect during construction through the early incentive program, the city page lists Commission-approved maximums of $4,045 for wastewater and $6,655 for water and wastewater, plus a 30-year payment plan and a hardship program. After that window, the city says owners would owe full construction and capacity costs, which it estimates could reach up to $35,000 based on similar projects. Contact North Port Utilities at 941-240-8000 for the current terms.

Separately, the city recently sent mandatory water-connection letters to about 450 homes near a water-line project, then the City Commission paused that requirement and directed staff to make the connection voluntary, with a cost cap to be set by the Commission. The city says new construction must still connect to city water where it’s available, and that homeowners who connect can keep their well for irrigation only. If you’re buying in North Port, it’s worth asking the utility whether any connection notices apply to that address.

Questions to ask before you buy

  1. Is the home on a well, city water, or both (for example, city water with a well for irrigation)?
  2. Is it on septic or central sewer? What does the Water & Sewer Availability Lookup show?
  3. What do the county septic records show, and when was the tank last pumped or serviced?
  4. Is it a conventional system or an ATU with an operating permit and maintenance contract?
  5. Has the well been tested recently by a certified lab, and can you see the results?
  6. Is the property in a planned sewer expansion area, or has it received any connection notice?

First-month checklist if you move into a well-and-septic home

  1. Find and mark the septic tank lid and drainfield so no one parks or builds on them.
  2. Get a well water test from the county health department or a certified lab if you don’t have recent results.
  3. Save the North Port Utilities (941-240-8000) and county Environmental Health (941-861-6133 or 941-861-3310) numbers.
  4. If you have an ATU, confirm the operating permit is current and the maintenance contract is in your name.
  5. After a hurricane or flooding, ask the health department about well testing before you drink the water.
