Change-of-address checklist for North Port & Venice newcomers

The boxes are in, the Wi-Fi works — now the paperwork. Missing one address update can mean a lost tax refund, a missed jury summons, or a registration renewal mailed to your old state. Here’s a practical order to work through it, with links to the official place to make each change.

Educational overview only, not legal or tax advice. Details below come from USPS, the IRS (Topic 157), and Florida Statutes §322.19. Fees, forms and procedures change — confirm with each agency before you rely on a deadline.

1. USPS mail forwarding (do this first)

  • Submit a change of address online at usps.com or at a Post Office. Choose permanent or temporary (handy for snowbirds splitting the year).
  • USPS requires identity verification. Online, it charges a small identity-verification fee, and the card’s billing address must match your old or new address — check the current amount on the USPS page.
  • USPS says to allow at least 7–10 business days for forwarding to start, and standard forwarding lasts 12 months (you can pay to extend it).
  • Forwarding is a safety net, not a fix: not everything is forwarded, and USPS itself recommends notifying senders directly.

2. Florida driver license / ID: 30-day rule

Under §322.19, if you change the residence or mailing address on a Florida license or ID card, you must get a replacement that shows the new address within 30 calendar days. If you’re coming from another state, converting to a Florida license is its own process — see our Florida driver license & car registration guide. In Sarasota County, driver license and tag services are handled through the Sarasota County Tax Collector’s offices; check their site or FLHSMV for appointments and accepted documents.

3. Vehicle registration, title and boat/trailer tags

  • Update the address on every vehicle, trailer and vessel registration so renewal notices reach you.
  • Tell your auto insurer — your garaging address affects your policy, and Florida requires proof of Florida insurance to register.

4. Voter registration

Moving within Florida or registering for the first time here? Update or register so your precinct and sample ballot are correct. Our voter registration guide walks through it with the Sarasota County Supervisor of Elections.

5. Federal agencies

  • IRS: the IRS says to file Form 8822 (or 8822-B for a business), or write to them, or use your new address on your next return. Because not all post offices forward government checks, notify the IRS directly rather than relying on forwarding. See Topic 157.
  • Social Security: if you receive benefits or Medicare, update your address with SSA — many people can do it through a my Social Security account.
  • Veterans: update your contact information in your VA.gov profile so benefits letters and health-care mail follow you. Ask the VA how the move affects which facility serves you.
  • Passport: you don’t need to update a passport for an address change, but use your new address on the next renewal.

6. Money, insurance and subscriptions

  • Banks, credit cards, retirement and brokerage accounts — mismatched addresses can trigger fraud holds on card purchases.
  • Employer payroll / HR (your W-2 goes to the address on file) and your state tax situation if you’re leaving a state with income tax — ask a tax professional.
  • Homeowners, flood, auto, life and health insurance. New Florida homeowners should also read our insurance basics and flood zones primer.
  • Doctors, pharmacies, vet, schools, and online stores (Amazon, subscriptions) — update saved shipping addresses so deliveries don’t go to the old house.

7. Local, home-specific items

  • Water, electric and trash accounts in your name — see utilities for new residents and trash & recycling.
  • Bought a home you’ll live in? The homestead exemption application uses your Florida license, registration and voter info, so getting steps 2–4 done helps.
  • Check that your mailing address is correct with the Sarasota County Property Appraiser so your property tax bill reaches you.
  • HOA or condo association — give them your mailing address and emergency contacts. See HOA first month.
  • Pets: update microchip registry info and your county pet license.

Watch for scams

Search results and ads sometimes lead to third-party “change of address” sites that charge extra fees. Make the USPS change on usps.com or at a Post Office, and type agency web addresses yourself rather than clicking ads.
