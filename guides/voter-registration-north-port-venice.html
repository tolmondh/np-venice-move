Registering to vote & updating your address after moving to North Port or Venice

Whether you're new to Florida or just moved here from another Florida county, your voter record needs your new Sarasota County address. North Port and Venice are both in Sarasota County, so the Sarasota County Supervisor of Elections handles your registration, your voter information card, and any vote-by-mail request. Here's what newcomers should do and where to check.

Educational overview only, not legal advice. Rules below come from the Florida Division of Elections and the Sarasota County Supervisor of Elections. Election dates and deadlines change every cycle, so confirm them with the Supervisor of Elections before you rely on them.

Who can register in Florida

The state says you must be a U.S. citizen, a Florida resident, and at least 18 (16- and 17-year-olds can pre-register). You also can't have been adjudicated mentally incapacitated with respect to voting, or convicted of a felony, without your rights restored. If any of that applies to you, the Division of Elections has details at dos.fl.gov/felon.

New to Florida: how to register

  • Online: at RegisterToVoteFlorida.gov, the state's official site.
  • When you get your Florida driver license: any tax collector office that issues licenses or ID cards can take your voter registration at the same visit. If you haven't switched your license yet, see our driver license & car registration guide and knock out both at once.
  • By mail or in person: with the statewide voter registration application (Form DS-DE 39), which you can get from the Division of Elections, a Supervisor of Elections office, or a local library.

Florida doesn't move your old state's registration for you. Once you're registered here, it's good practice to contact your previous state or county election office to cancel your old registration.

Moved here from another Florida county

The state says you update your record (address, name, or party) using any of the same options above. Do it as soon as you have your new address so your precinct, polling place, and any mail ballot go to the right place. If you were issued a new Florida driver license or ID number since you registered, the Division of Elections asks you to contact the Supervisor of Elections so your voter record shows the latest number.

Deadlines to know

  • Registering: the deadline to register for an upcoming election is 29 days before that election. The same 29-day deadline applies to changing party before a primary.
  • Florida has closed primaries: in partisan primary races, generally only voters registered with that party can vote. Pick or change your party before the deadline if that matters to you.
  • Moved close to an election? If a deadline has already passed, call the Supervisor of Elections and ask what your options are for your situation rather than guessing.
  • Current election dates are on the state's Election Dates page.

Voter information card

After you register or update, the Supervisor of Elections mails you a voter information card showing your party, precinct, polling place, and districts. It's also mailed again when your polling place changes. Florida says the card can't be used as ID at the polls. If it doesn't arrive, call the Sarasota County office for a replacement; the state doesn't issue them.

Vote-by-mail after a move

  • Request it in your new county: requests go to the Supervisor of Elections where you're registered, so a request you made in another county doesn't carry over. You can request online, by phone, in person, or in writing on Form DS-DE 160.
  • New address? The state says a signed written request is required if the address you want the ballot sent to is different from the one on file.
  • Requests expire: a request covers elections through the end of the calendar year of the next regularly scheduled general election, then you need to renew it.
  • Timing: the request deadline to have a ballot mailed is 5 p.m. on the 12th day before the election, and a voted ballot must be received by 7 p.m. on Election Day, not just postmarked.
  • Returned mail cancels it: if a mail ballot comes back undeliverable, your request is canceled, which is one more reason to update your address first.

Full rules are on the state's Vote-by-Mail page.

Sarasota County Supervisor of Elections offices

All three offices list hours of Monday through Friday, 8:30 a.m. to 5 p.m. on the county's Office Locations page. Check there before you go, since hours and sites can shift around elections.

  • North Port: North Port Village, 14277 S Tamiami Trail, North Port, FL 34287. Phone 941-423-9540. (This replaced the older Biscayne Plaza office, so ignore that address if you see it online.)
  • Venice: Robert L. Anderson Administration Center, 4000 S Tamiami Trail, Room 1070, Venice, FL 34293. Phone 941-861-3760.
  • Sarasota: Terrace Building, 2001 Adams Lane, Sarasota, FL 34237. Phone 941-861-8600.

First-month checklist

  1. Register or update your address online, or at the tax collector when you get your Florida license.
  2. Choose your party (or no party) knowing that Florida primaries are closed.
  3. Watch for your voter information card and check your precinct and polling place.
  4. If you vote by mail, submit a new request in Sarasota County.
  5. Cancel your registration in your previous state or county.

Where to verify

  • Sarasota County Supervisor of Elections and its office locations.
  • Florida Division of Elections: Register to vote or update your information.
  • Florida Division of Elections: Vote-by-mail.
  • RegisterToVoteFlorida.gov.
