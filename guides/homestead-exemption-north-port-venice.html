Homestead exemption for new North Port & Venice homeowners

If you buy a home in North Port or Venice and make it your permanent residence, Florida's homestead exemption can lower the taxable value of that home and limit how fast its assessed value rises in later years. You have to apply for it; it isn't automatic. This page walks newcomers through the timing, the paperwork, and where to file in Sarasota County.

Educational overview only, not tax or legal advice. We don't estimate tax bills or savings. Exemption amounts, eligibility rules, and deadlines are set by Florida law and administered by the Sarasota County Property Appraiser. Confirm everything there before you file.

The two dates that matter

  • January 1: According to the Sarasota County Property Appraiser, to qualify for a given year you must be a Florida resident and own and occupy the home as your permanent residence on January 1 of that year. If you close and move in during, say, the fall, you'd generally be applying for the following year.
  • March 1: The last day to file a timely application for that year. The Property Appraiser's office says it will still consider applications filed after March 1, but don't count on a late filing; apply as soon as you're eligible.

Many buyers file right after closing and moving in, so the paperwork is done well before the deadline. The Property Appraiser lets you apply online and check your application status.

Do these first: driver license, voter registration, and records

Sarasota County asks for evidence that the home is your permanent Florida residence, and the Florida driver license step often takes the longest for people moving from out of state. A practical order:

  1. Get a Florida driver license or ID card showing the new address. The Property Appraiser lists this as required for all applicants, and says a "Valid in Florida Only" license or ID isn't acceptable. Start with FLHSMV for appointments and document requirements.
  2. Register to vote (or update your address) in Sarasota County, or use one of the other forms of residency evidence the Property Appraiser accepts, such as a recorded declaration of domicile.
  3. Deal with your previous home. If you still own your prior home, the Property Appraiser says you'll need proof that any exemption or similar benefit there was canceled. If you sold it, you'll need proof of the sale date.
  4. Gather ownership documents. Your deed will usually already be in the county's records by the time you apply, but having a copy of the recorded deed is helpful.

Documents Sarasota County asks for

Based on the Property Appraiser's homestead overview (check it for the current list):

  • Evidence of ownership, such as a recorded deed (or, in some cases, a proprietary lease, trust agreement, or other document showing beneficial interest).
  • A valid Florida driver license or Florida ID card that shows the permanent residence address.
  • Additional proof of permanent residence, such as Florida voter registration at the address, plus information about your previous residence.
  • Social Security numbers. The Property Appraiser notes Florida law requires them, and married applicants must provide information for both spouses, even if only one is on the deed.
  • For permanent resident aliens who aren't U.S. citizens, a permanent resident card (green card).

The office may ask for more information to verify your status, so keep copies of what you submit.

Save Our Homes: the assessed-value cap

Once a homestead exemption is in place, Florida's Save Our Homes rule limits how much the home's assessed value can rise each year. The Sarasota County Property Appraiser describes the cap as no more than 3% or the change in the Consumer Price Index, whichever is lower, beginning the year after the homestead is established. That's a limit on assessed value, not a promise about your total tax bill, which also depends on tax rates and non-ad valorem assessments.

Already have a Florida homestead? Ask about portability

If you're moving to North Port or Venice from another Florida home that had a homestead exemption, you may be able to transfer some or all of your Save Our Homes benefit to the new home. Each situation is different, so read the Save Our Homes and portability page and the portability FAQ, and mention your prior homestead when you apply.

Other exemptions you might qualify for

The Property Appraiser lists additional exemptions and discounts for some homeowners, including certain seniors age 65 and older with limited income, some veterans with service-connected disabilities, and some people with total and permanent disabilities. Each has its own requirements and paperwork. If any of these might apply to you, read the details on the exemptions page or call the office before you file.

Common newcomer mistakes to avoid

  • Assuming the exemption carries over from the seller. It's tied to the owner, so you file your own application.
  • Waiting on the Florida driver license until after the filing deadline.
  • Keeping a residency-based exemption or benefit on a home in another state or county. Ask both places how to cancel it properly.
  • Renting out the home without checking how that affects the exemption. The Property Appraiser has a page on ways to lose your homestead.

Where to verify

  • Sarasota County Property Appraiser: Homestead, for eligibility, documents, the online application, and status checks.
  • Florida Department of Revenue: property tax exemptions, for the statewide rules.
  • Sarasota County Tax Collector, for paying property tax bills and driver license and vehicle services.
  • FLHSMV, for Florida driver license and vehicle registration requirements.
