Pest control

Southwest Florida homes deal with termites, palmetto bugs (American cockroaches), ants, and rodents year-round. When you buy, it is worth asking whether a recent wood-destroying organism (WDO) inspection was done and whether any termite warranty can transfer. After you move in, ask any company you interview what is included in a regular service, how they treat around kids and pets, and what happens after a hurricane when pests are more active.

Pest control businesses in Florida are licensed by the Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services (FDACS), not by the Department of Business and Professional Regulation. Check the company — and, when you can, the technician — in FDACS’s pest control database before you sign a contract.

Official resources: FDACS: How do I know if a pest control company is licensed? · FDACS Pest Control Database Search

Pool service

A pool is common here, and the climate means it needs regular attention. Weekly service is typical. Ask what is included (chemicals, filter cleaning, equipment checks), whether the pool is chlorine or salt, and who is responsible if a pump or heater fails. If you live in an HOA or condo, read the rules on screening, maintenance, and storm prep — some associations require the pool to be kept in service.

Before a hurricane, local emergency guidance usually includes lowering the water to the right level, adding chlorine, and bringing in loose furniture. Confirm current instructions with the city when a storm approaches. Building, remodeling, or repairing a pool often requires a licensed residential pool/spa contractor or servicing contractor. Look the license up before work starts.

Official resources: Florida DBPR license search (myfloridalicense.com) · HOA first-month guide · Hurricane prep for newcomers

HVAC / air conditioning

Air conditioning runs most of the year on this coast. Before you hire anyone, ask when the system was last serviced, what filter it uses, and whether the equipment was sized for the house. A poorly maintained system is one of the first things newcomers notice in summer humidity.

Many air-conditioning, mechanical, and electrical jobs require a licensed contractor in Florida. After a storm, be cautious of unsolicited door-to-door offers. Verify the company on the official DBPR license search, and ask the city or county whether a permit is required before a replacement or major repair.

Official resources: Florida DBPR license search (myfloridalicense.com) · DBPR Construction Industry · City of North Port · City of Venice · Sarasota County

Lawn and landscaping

Warm-season grasses grow quickly in the rainy months, and many yards here use irrigation from a well or from reclaimed water. Cities and the county set irrigation days and hours; those rules change with the season and with water-shortage orders, so confirm the current schedule with North Port, Venice, or Sarasota County rather than copying a neighbor.

If a company applies pesticides or certain fertilizers, Florida may require an FDACS license for that work. Landscape installation, irrigation, or tree work can also require a contractor license or a local permit. Ask what is included in a monthly service, whether they will follow watering restrictions, and how they handle HOA appearance rules if you have them.

Water treatment

If you are on city water, start with the utility’s water-quality information and its annual Consumer Confidence Report. North Port and Venice both publish water-quality pages. If the home is on a private well — common on many older North Port lots — testing and treatment are your responsibility. Our well water and septic guide walks through records, testing, and city sewer expansion.

Softeners and filters are often used for hardness or a sulfur smell on some wells. Ask what the equipment is treating, how it is maintained, and whether a licensed plumber is doing any plumbing connections. Confirm current utility contacts on the official city or county site, not on an old blog post.

Official resources: North Port Utilities: Water Quality · City of Venice: Water Quality · North Port Utilities Department · Utilities setup guide · DBPR license search (for licensed plumbers)

Movers and storage

Companies that move household goods inside Florida must register with FDACS. The department says you should confirm that registration first, get a written estimate and contract before any work starts, and be cautious of a quote that looks far too low. FDACS also warns about “hostage move” scams, where the price jumps after the truck is loaded. Interstate moves are regulated at the federal level; check the mover with the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration.

If you need a storage unit during the move, climate control matters in this humidity, and you should ask how the facility prepares for hurricane season. See our storage guide for the questions to ask.

Official resources: FDACS: Moving Within Florida · FDACS Business License Lookup · FMCSA: Protect Your Move (interstate) · Storage during a move

Handyman

A handyman can be a help with painting, TV mounts, and small repairs after you move in. Many jobs, though, are reserved for licensed contractors in Florida — including a good deal of electrical, plumbing, roofing, HVAC, and structural work — and some projects need a building permit. If the work sounds like more than a minor fix, check the license at myfloridalicense.com and ask the City of North Port, the City of Venice, or Sarasota County whether a permit is required before anyone starts.

Official resources: Florida DBPR license search (myfloridalicense.com) · City of North Port · City of Venice · Sarasota County

Cleaning

A move-in or move-out cleaning is one of the first things many newcomers book, especially if the home was vacant or just renovated. There is generally no statewide professional license just for residential house cleaning. Ask for a local business tax receipt, proof of insurance, and references, and get the scope of work in writing (inside only, appliances, windows, and so on).

If a company also applies pesticides, does mold remediation, or handles restoration after a leak, different state licenses may apply — use the FDACS or DBPR lookups linked on this page, depending on the work.

Official resources: Florida DBPR license search · FDACS Business License Lookup · City or county business-tax information via North Port, Venice, or Sarasota County
